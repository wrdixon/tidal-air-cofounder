Department and Workstream Map

Maps the full shape of the company independent of current headcount. One person may own multiple workstreams early. Purpose is to understand scope and eventual ownership.
Overview
01 Product Hardware + Software

Hardware and software sit under a single product umbrella because the two are inseparable — the dashboard has no value without the unit, and the unit's differentiation depends on what the software can report and control.

Hardware engineering
Physical design of the unit: form factor, acoustics, filtration geometry, fan selection, sensor integration, and ongoing hardware revisions based on what pilots reveal.
Firmware
Software running on the unit: fan control, sensor reading, airflow and eACH calculation, filter life prediction, and OTA updates. Firmware reliability determines whether the platform's data can be trusted.
Software and dashboard
Cloud platform: fleet management, per-room IAQ reporting, remote control, and alert delivery. The current pilot dashboard will need to be rebuilt for reliability and scale before broad deployment.
Regulatory and compliance
UL, FCC, noise validation against ANSI S12.60, and the basis for ASHRAE 241 compliance claims. Also tracks evolving state and federal IAQ standards. Lives in Product because compliance claims need to stay close to the engineers who validate them.
02 Supply Chain and Manufacturing Ops

Intentionally separated from product engineering because the skills diverge — product design is creative and technical, supply chain is operational. Leaving supply chain under engineering too long is a common early hardware company mistake.

Pilot assembly
In-house unit builds to support the first wave of deployments before a CM is qualified. Temporary by design.
CM management
Selection, qualification, and ongoing management of the contract manufacturer. Includes work instructions, yield targets, ramp schedules, and the day-to-day factory relationship.
QC and testing
Incoming component inspection and outgoing unit inspection. Owns the performance standard regardless of where testing physically happens.
3PL and inventory
Warehousing and fulfillment through a third-party logistics provider. Covers the 3PL relationship, demand forecasting, and filter replenishment logistics.
03 Sales and Business Development

The go-to-market model is not fully resolved — direct, channel, or both remain open. Regardless of model, someone at Tidal Air owns the commercial relationship with the customer. These workstreams exist under either approach.

Direct sales
Consultative sale to school districts, owned end-to-end by Tidal Air. Slower to scale but produces deeper relationships and better early product feedback.
Channel and resellers
Selling through partners — low-voltage contractors, AV integrators, or facilities distributors — who have existing district relationships. Covers partner recruiting, pricing structure, and enablement. May or may not be part of the initial model but will likely be part of the model at scale.
Marketing and positioning
Product brief, website, case studies, and content that supports the sales process. At this stage closely tied to sales; expands to inbound and category-level messaging over time.
04 Partners and Deployment

Owns physical installation and the partner network that performs it. The model will evolve from hands-on early installs — to learn what we need to learn — toward a certified partner network. Whether partners are pure installers or sell-and-install integrators is an open question; this department owns the relationship either way. The most natural partner type is low-voltage or AV contractors: already in school buildings, familiar with ceiling work, and typically on district approved vendor lists.

Installer and partner network
Recruiting, certification, training, and ongoing partner relationship management. Certification standards need to be tight enough to catch commissioning errors before they show up as bad data in a customer's dashboard.
Installation standards
Ceiling mount specs, wiring standards, commissioning procedures, and room registration process. These are what the certification program is built on.
Pilot deployments
Tidal Air staff do the first installs directly — not as a permanent model, but to understand what installation actually involves before writing standards and training partners against them.
05 Customer Success

Owns the district relationship after installation. Distinct from sales (pre-contract) and from partners and deployment (install). The recurring filter relationship and ongoing software relationship make retention a real function.

Onboarding
Dashboard setup, room configuration, and training facilities staff from go-live through independent operation.
Ongoing support
Support tickets, filter replacement logistics, SLA management, and proactive outreach driven by platform data. Our units self-report status, so support can be proactive rather than reactive.
Renewals and expansion
Contract renewals and expansion to additional buildings. Will be handled by the same person as onboarding and support early on, but is a distinct function worth naming.
06 G&A

At seed stage, mostly founder-owned with outside counsel and fractional finance support as needed.

Fundraising
Investor narrative, financial model, outreach, diligence, and close. A significant time commitment that runs in parallel with everything else.
Legal and contracts
Entity setup, customer and vendor agreements, and IP including patent filings. ASHRAE compliance claims and noise certification language create real IP surface area worth protecting.
Finance and operations
Bookkeeping, payroll, and basic financial reporting. Handled externally where possible.
Hiring
Recruiting and closing early hires and co-founder candidates. Highly strategic at this stage — each of the first few hires materially changes what the company can do.
