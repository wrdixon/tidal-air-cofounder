Why now

This is why we are building Tidal Air.

Biosecurity threats from AI and synthetic biology represent a serious and growing risk, and indoor air filtration is one of the most practical ways to reduce respiratory disease transmission at scale. It is also why we are building Tidal Air. We are not alone in this concern, with the OpenAI Foundation, Coefficient Giving, and ARPA-H among organizations committing hundreds of millions of dollars per year to biosecurity infrastructure, and that number is growing.

Fortunately, clean air in buildings has significant value well beyond pandemic preparedness. A growing body of research links indoor air quality to cognitive performance, respiratory health, and productivity, and that evidence base is expanding rapidly. Building compliance frameworks including WELL, RESET, and LEED are beginning to require verified continuous air quality measurement and the global portable air purifier market has reached $12.8 billion in 2025, growing at nearly 7% annually and now larger than at any point during the COVID-19 pandemic.

The conditions to meaningfully scale indoor air purification are here now.

The beachhead

Schools first. Every building is the goal.

With over 3 million classrooms in the US and the 100 largest districts responsible for roughly 1 in 4 students nationally, schools represent a concentrated and scalable market.

Schools also have immediate reasons to care: wildfire smoke is now an annual reality across much of the country, asthma is the leading cause of chronic absenteeism, research increasingly links particulate matter exposure to drops in attendance and test scores, and there is growing evidence that cleaner air meaningfully reduces the spread of seasonal illness.

Since COVID, districts have been spending on this problem. But current air purifier offerings, often mass rollouts of residential units, are an exceptionally poor fit for large-scale institutional deployments. The three largest adoption blockers are:

Too Loud to Actually Use

Units are marketed at maximum fan speeds which are loud enough to disrupt instruction. Teachers turn them down or off entirely.

High Operating Costs

Proprietary filter replacements are a core revenue source for air purifier brands, with filter costs often exceeding the original hardware investment within two years.

Unmanageable at Scale

There is no way to manage multiple units at once or verify that air quality standards are actually being met.

What we're building

The enterprise version of air purification.

Tidal Air is to portable air purifiers what enterprise wifi is to a home router: centrally visible, remotely manageable, and built to cover an entire campus from a single interface.

Ceiling-mounted. Always on.

Out of reach, out of the way, and quiet enough that nobody turns it off. Meets classroom acoustic standards at full performance.

Standard filters. No lock-in.

Commodity HVAC filters from any supplier. Replaced when a pressure sensor says they are needed, not on a calendar.

Every room. Live data.

Each unit continuously measures PM2.5, CO2, CADR, and filter life. A single dashboard shows every room across an entire district.

Taken together, every room gets a continuous verified answer to whether filtered air delivery is sufficient to reduce pathogen spread, not just whether a purifier is running.

Engineered Different

Designed for classrooms, not living rooms.

Portable air purifiers were designed for consumer use, optimized for aesthetics and proprietary filter revenue rather than institutional performance. Manufacturers report performance at maximum fan speed, which no teacher would tolerate, so the specs that look impressive on the box have little bearing on what actually happens in a classroom.

We started with different constraints. The result is a first prototype that delivers more clean air at classroom-appropriate noise levels, runs on a fraction of the power, and costs 80% less to maintain. The unit in the comparison below was built specifically to test this in a comparable form factor, and there is significant room to improve from here.
