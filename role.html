The work

What the work looks like right now

Working prototypes are deployed and I'm preparing to run the first school pilots. These are early hardware going in front of real schools to learn what actually works, what teachers and facilities staff respond to, and what to build next. The technical foundation is solid enough to start learning. Everything on the outside of the company still needs to be built. That's the job.

Figure out the sales motion. I have a hypothesis about who buys this and why, but the real answers come from being in front of districts, watching what resonates, and updating quickly. You'll build that playbook from scratch.

Build the funding playbook for districts. Air purification isn't a line item most school budgets have room for. Part of the job is understanding what funding mechanisms actually exist: grants, state allocations, and a growing number of biosecurity-focused funders including the OpenAI Foundation and ARPA-H, which are committing hundreds of millions per year to this space. If we can demonstrate this at scale, Advanced Market Commitments become a real possibility. Being able to hand a district a clear path to yes is core to closing deals.

Lay the groundwork for distribution. At some point direct sales doesn't scale. The right installers, facilities contractors, or channel partners are out there. You won't build that in the first year, but you'll start.

Everything else. The work won't stay inside these lines, and some weeks the job is just figuring out what the job is. The ability to context-switch and stay useful when the problem changes is as important as anything on this list.
