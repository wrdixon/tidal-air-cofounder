Co-Founder Opportunity

Clean air should be infrastructure,
not an afterthought.

Tidal Air is building fleet-managed air purification infrastructure for the 3+ million classrooms in the US, with a larger mission to make clean air permanent building infrastructure before the next pandemic.

We have working prototypes deployed and our pre-seed is being led by Halcyon Futures with Geoff Ralston (former YC president) and Matt Rogers (Nest co-founder) as angels. We are looking for a non-technical co-founder to own school and district relationships, pilot execution, and sales from the ground up. Boston-based and early-stage experience preferred.

Why now

The understanding of indoor air has shifted.

A growing body of research links indoor air quality to cognitive performance, respiratory health, and productivity in ways that were not well quantified a decade ago. The effect shows up in schools, offices, and homes alike. The global portable air purifier market reflects this: $12.8 billion in 2025 and growing at nearly 7% annually, driven largely by residential adoption as awareness has spread.

Wildfire smoke has accelerated that shift, making the problem concrete and visible in communities that might otherwise have moved slowly. On top of that underlying growth, biosecurity threats from AI and synthetic biology are driving a new wave of institutional funding. The OpenAI Foundation and Coefficient Giving are among the foundations committing hundreds of millions of dollars per year to pandemic preparedness infrastructure. ARPA-H launched its BREATHE program to build smart building systems for indoor air. Compliance frameworks including WELL, RESET, LEED, and tightening state ventilation standards are beginning to require verified, continuous air quality measurement.

The beachhead

Schools first. Every building is the goal.

There are over 3 million classrooms in the US and the 100 largest districts are responsible for roughly 1 in 4 students nationally, meaning a single district relationship can represent tens of thousands of deployed units. Schools have immediate reasons to care: wildfire smoke is now an annual reality across much of the country, asthma is the leading cause of chronic absenteeism, and research increasingly links particulate matter exposure to drops in attendance and test scores.

Districts have already been spending heavily on this problem. The challenge is that existing solutions do not hold up in practice.

Too loud.

Most units only meet classroom acoustic standards at their lowest settings, delivering a fraction of rated performance. Teachers turn them down or off entirely.

Captive filter costs.

The business model is proprietary consumables, the same way printer companies profit from cartridges. Filter costs exceed the original hardware within two years, and when replacement costs hit individual building budgets, maintenance stops.

No visibility.

These are individual appliances with no network, no management layer, and no data. There is no way to know whether the investment is doing anything.

What we're building

The enterprise version of air purification.

Tidal Air is to portable air purifiers what enterprise wifi is to a home router: centrally visible, remotely manageable, and built to cover an entire campus from a single interface.

Ceiling-mounted. Always on.

Out of reach, out of the way, and quiet enough that nobody turns it off. Meets classroom acoustic standards at full performance.

Standard filters. No lock-in.

Commodity HVAC filters from any supplier. Replaced when a differential pressure sensor says they are needed, not on a calendar.

Every room. Live data.

Each unit continuously measures PM2.5, CO2, CADR, and filter life. A single dashboard shows every room across an entire district.

Head to head

More airflow. Less noise. 80% lower filter costs.

Our first prototype, unoptimized, already outperforms the top-rated consumer unit on airflow at comparable noise levels, at a fraction of the power and maintenance cost.
