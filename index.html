Why now

The understanding of indoor air has shifted.

The global portable air purifier market has reached $12.8 billion in 2025, growing at nearly 7% annually and now larger than at any point during the COVID-19 pandemic. The growth is driven largely by residential adoption as awareness of indoor air quality has increased.

A growing body of research links indoor air quality to cognitive performance, respiratory health, and productivity in ways that were not well quantified a decade ago. Building compliance frameworks including WELL, RESET, LEED, and tightening state ventilation standards are beginning to require verified, continuous air quality measurement.

Biosecurity threats from AI and synthetic biology are driving a new wave of institutional funding on top of that underlying growth, and those threats are only becoming more serious. The OpenAI Foundation, Coefficient Giving, and ARPA-H are among a number of organizations committing hundreds of millions of dollars per year to building new biosecurity infrastructure.

The beachhead

Schools first. Every building is the goal.

With over 3 million classrooms in the US and the 100 largest districts responsible for roughly 1 in 4 students nationally, schools represent a concentrated and scalable market. Schools also have immediate reasons to care: wildfire smoke is now an annual reality across much of the country, asthma is the leading cause of chronic absenteeism, research increasingly links particulate matter exposure to drops in attendance and test scores, and there is growing evidence that cleaner air meaningfully reduces the spread of seasonal illness.

Since COVID, districts have been spending on this problem. But current air purifier offerings, often mass rollouts of residential units, are an exceptionally poor fit for large-scale institutional deployments. The three largest adoption blockers are:

Too loud to actually use.

These units are marketed at high performance numbers that are only achievable at fan speeds loud enough to disrupt instruction. Teachers turn them down to the point where they deliver a fraction of rated performance, or off entirely.

Unsustainable operating costs.

Proprietary filter replacements are a core revenue source for air purifier brands, with filter costs exceeding the original hardware investment within two years. Schools simply do not have the budget to keep up with maintenance.

Unmanageable at scale.

Current purifiers are individual appliances with no fleet management layer and no analytics. There is no way to manage hundreds of units at scale or verify that air quality standards are actually being met.

What we're building

The enterprise version of air purification.

Tidal Air is to portable air purifiers what enterprise wifi is to a home router: centrally visible, remotely manageable, and built to cover an entire campus from a single interface.

Ceiling-mounted. Always on.

Out of reach, out of the way, and quiet enough that nobody turns it off. Meets classroom acoustic standards at full performance.

Standard filters. No lock-in.

Commodity HVAC filters from any supplier. Replaced when a pressure sensor says they are needed, not on a calendar.

Every room. Live data.

Each unit continuously measures PM2.5, CO2, CADR, and filter life. A single dashboard shows every room across an entire district.
